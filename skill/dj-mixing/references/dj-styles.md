# DJ styles

A DJ's style decides which techniques fit, how long transitions last, how tempo and genre change, and what "good" sounds like. Identify the style before you choose any technique. If the user doesn't name one, infer it from the context (a wedding → mobile/event; a techno set → genre specialist) and state your assumption.

## Quick comparison

| Style | Genre range | Typical transition | BPM handling | Energy shape | Signature techniques |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Genre specialist | One genre | Long (16–64 bars) | Tight range, small drifts | Slow, deep waves | Long blends, bass swaps, layering, harmonic mixing |
| Club DJ | A few related genres | Medium–long (8–32 bars) | Steady, rising toward peak | Builds to peak time, waves | Bass swaps, filter builds, drop swaps, double drops |
| Turntablist / scratch DJ | Any; often hip-hop, funk, breaks | Short, performative | Often cut-based | Showcase moments | Scratches, beat juggling, cuts, trick mixing |
| Mobile and event DJ | Very broad, all ages | Short–medium (4–16 bars) or clean cuts | Wide; bridged carefully | Follows the event timeline | Clean cuts, short blends, echo outs, fades |
| Radio DJ | Format-dependent | Segues, cold endings, talk-overs | Rarely beatmatched | Steady, consistent | Segues, voice-over ducking, timed posts |
| Open-format DJ | Everything | Short (1–8 bars) or cuts | Large jumps, bridged | Reacts to the room | Cuts, echo outs, BPM bridging, edits, quick hitters |

## Genre specialist

Focuses deeply on one sound (techno, drum & bass, house) within a tight tempo range.

- **Goal:** a continuous, immersive flow where transitions are hard to notice.
- **Transitions:** long blends (16–64 bars), bass swaps, EQ swaps, layering two tracks for extended passages.
- **Tempo:** stays within a few BPM; changes slowly over the set.
- **Key:** harmonic mixing matters a lot because overlaps are long.
- **Energy:** slow, hypnotic waves rather than sudden jumps.
- **Avoid:** hard cuts, gimmick effects, genre jumps.
- **Genre notes:** techno favours very long layered blends; drum & bass favours fast bass swaps and double drops; house favours 16–32 bar blends over DJ intros and outros.

## Club DJ

Curates high-energy dance sets for nightlife venues and late-night crowds.

- **Goal:** keep the floor moving and build toward peak-time moments.
- **Transitions:** 8–32 bar blends, bass swaps on phrase boundaries, filter and roll builds into drops, drop swaps and occasional double drops at peak time.
- **Tempo:** steady, often rising gently from warm-up to peak.
- **Energy:** follow the arcs in set-building.md: warm-up → peak waves → release.
- **Avoid:** long low-energy stretches at peak time; too many effect-heavy transitions in a row.

## Turntablist / scratch DJ

Uses turntables as instruments: complex scratches, beat juggling and trick mixing.

- **Goal:** showcase skill; the transition is a performance moment in itself.
- **Transitions:** cuts on the downbeat, scratch-in intros, beat juggles between two copies of a break, quick back-and-forth cuts, spinbacks and brakes.
- **Material:** breaks, acapellas, battle records and short samples.
- **Rendering note:** these are rendered as rate curves and gate patterns (see techniques.md, section 8). A rendered scratch can approximate a pattern but not the nuance of a skilled hand; use them as accents, and tell the user this is a simulation.
- **Avoid:** long blends; overusing the same pattern.

## Mobile and event DJ

Plays private parties, corporate events and weddings, using a broad open-format style to please all ages.

- **Goal:** the right music for each moment of the event, for everyone present.
- **Event timeline:** plan the set around the event's phases, for example arrival/cocktails (background, low energy), dinner (background), key moments (entrance, first dance, speeches, cake), open dancing (rising energy, crowd favours across decades), last song.
- **Transitions:** clean, safe and quick: short blends, phrase cuts, echo outs, fades for key moments.
- **Content:** prefer clean (radio) edits; avoid explicit lyrics unless the user says otherwise.
- **Tempo and genre:** wide range across decades; bridge carefully (see BPM bridging below).
- **Loudness:** keep it very consistent; guests notice jumps more than clubbers do.
- **Avoid:** long experimental passages, aggressive effects, obscure tracks during dancing.

## Radio DJ

Curates broadcasts, talks on air and manages rotation playlists rather than live mixing.

- **Goal:** consistent, polished flow that fits strict timing.
- **Transitions:**
  - Segue: the next track starts as the last one ends, sometimes with a short overlap but no beatmatching.
  - Cold ending vs. fade ending: let tracks with real endings end; overlap tracks that fade.
  - Talk-over: a voice segment over an instrumental intro, ending exactly as the vocal starts (the "post"). Use the intro length up to the first vocal from analysis.
- **Mixing:** duck the music under any voice track by about 10–15 dB, with quick attack and slower release.
- **Timing:** hit hard time points (top of the hour, ad breaks) by trimming or extending tracks.
- **Content:** radio edits; loudness normalized to the broadcast target.
- **Avoid:** long blends, beatmatched tricks, heavy effects.

## Open-format DJ

No fixed genre: a set can move through hip-hop, R&B, house, pop, Latin, funk, afrobeats and throwbacks.

- **Goal:** read the room and change energy or style the moment it's needed.
- **Crowd reading without a crowd:** you can't see a dance floor. Use what you can get: the user's description of the audience and event, explicit feedback between sets, requests, and the energy arc they want. Ask for this context when it matters, and say what you assumed when you can't.
- **Transitions:** short and decisive: cuts on the downbeat, 1–8 bar blends, echo outs, spinbacks, quick fades.
- **Quick hitters:** play only the most recognizable 30–90 seconds of a track (usually a hook or chorus) before moving on. Choose regions from section analysis.
- **Wordplay edits:** connect tracks through lyrics or titles: the next song answers, repeats or puns on a line from the previous one. This needs lyric or title data; only plan it when that data is available.
- **Avoid:** long blends between different genres; staying in one genre so long that part of the room disconnects.

### BPM bridging

Open-format and mobile DJs often cross big tempo and style gaps, such as a 90 BPM R&B track into a 124 BPM house track. Strategies, roughly from safest to boldest:

| Strategy | How it works | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phrase cut | End A on its last phrase; B starts on the next downbeat at its own tempo | Any gap; most reliable |
| Echo out | Echo A's last beat or word; B starts as the tail fades | Any gap; smooth feel |
| Acapella or intro bridge | Start B on a section without drums (acapella intro, pad intro), so tempo isn't heard until B's beat starts | Big gaps where you want continuity |
| Half/double-time | Use a 2:1 relationship if one exists (e.g. 62 ↔ 124, 87 ↔ 174) | Hip-hop ↔ house/D&B |
| Transition track | Insert a track whose tempo sits between, or a tempo-shift edit that changes speed inside the track | Gradual journeys |
| Tempo ramp | Ramp A's tempo toward B's over several phrases, with key lock | Gaps up to ~10% |
| Spinback or brake | Stop A dramatically; B starts fresh | Fun, high-energy moments |
| Silence break | A short stop before B | Big genre resets, surprises |

90 → 124 BPM is a 38% jump: never beatmatch it directly. Use a cut, an echo out, an acapella bridge or a transition track.

## Mixing styles within a set

Real DJs blend styles: a club DJ may play an open-format hour, a mobile DJ may run a club-style dance block. Choose a style per section of the set when the user asks for variety, and switch styles at a clear moment (a cut or a breakdown) rather than mid-transition.
